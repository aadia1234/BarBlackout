The hard part

There's no API for "is a video playing." So it's inferred from two.

macOS never exposed a public way to know what you're watching, and Apple locked down the private one (MediaRemote) in macOS 15.4. BarBlackout ANDs two public signals from the frontmost app instead:

IOKit

Holds a display-sleep assertion

Video players take one specifically for visual playback. Audio-only apps deliberately don't.

AND
CoreAudio

Is actively outputting audio

The per-process audio gate rejects presentations and screen-sharing, which take the same assertion silently.

=
Result

A video is playing

Pause or switch apps and the assertion drops — the bar returns instantly. Trade-off: a muted video won't register.

The fiddly part is browsers. Chrome plays media in renderer helper processes; Safari's WebContent processes are reparented and only link back via "responsible pid." BarBlackout walks both the process tree and the responsible-pid chain, so audio from a background tab is still attributed to the browser you're looking at.

What it does

An overlay, not a setting.

It draws a borderless black window one level above the menu bar — so the bar is never reconfigured, just covered.

01

Timed blackouts

Presets from 15 minutes to 4 hours, a custom slider up to 24h in 15-minute steps, or ∞ to hide indefinitely.

02

Context-aware modes

Always, desktop-only, full-screen-only, or only when a video is actually playing in full screen.

03

Hover to reveal

Move your cursor into the menu-bar zone and the overlay steps aside, then re-covers on mouse-out.

04

The icon is the timer

A ring that fills from its right edge as the countdown runs — empty while blacked out, a solid disc once the bar is back.

05

No permissions

Reads the active Space type straight from the window server — no Accessibility or Screen Recording prompts.

06

Stays out of the way

Runs as a background agent with no Dock icon, and touches nothing about your Mac's configuration.
